# words-1.6, the End button, and translations on two surfaces — research

_2026-08-20. Seven asks, four surfaces, one shared data gap._

The request, restated:

1. **The tutor must not ask questions**, and when something question-shaped slips out it must not
   wait for an answer — pause briefly and carry on.
2. **No "say it back", no "spell it", no "give me an example"** — the learner does not want to
   unmute and talk.
3. **The in-app End session button does not work.**
4. **The lock-screen card must show translations**, numbered, one word per line:
   `1 word - translation RU`.
5. **Remove the "Add the Pause and Mute controls…" line** from the card — it costs space.
6. **Move the card's status line onto the title line** — title left, status right.
7. **On the lesson page's Words panel**: each word links to its word page, each row shows its
   translation in the same numbered one-per-line format, and `remove` becomes a ✕ icon with a
   confirmation dialog.

(1) and (2) are one prompt version. (3) is a bug with a specific, provable cause. (4), (6) and (7)
are all blocked on the same missing data — the lesson's item rows carry **no word id and no
translation** — so they are one API change wearing three hats. (5) is a deletion with a cost worth
naming.

## §1 — words-1.6: 1.3 plus podcast pacing

### 1.1 Why 1.4 and 1.5 lost

Both are in the tree and both are the same experiment run twice. Read as a pair they say something
useful about what to keep:

- **1.4** (`docs/2026-08-17-short-turns-and-chunked-pause.md`) redefined the turn: at most four
  sentences (~60 words), one of five threads per turn, and **every turn must end by handing the
  floor back**. It also pinned `maxTokens: 220` as a backstop.
- **1.5** (`docs/2026-08-18-podcast-mode-tutor.md`) kept 1.4's chunking, deleted the hand-back,
  and added `turnTimeoutSeconds: 3` + `turnEagerness: "patient"` so the tutor resumes into silence
  by itself.

The verdict is that **1.3 teaches better**. That is consistent with what changed: 1.3's per-item
instruction is *"weave MEANING / TRANSLATION / FORMS / USAGE / SOUND into a natural spoken
explanation"*, and 1.4 replaced weaving with slicing — five separate turns that each have to
re-establish context, plus a `maxTokens: 220` ceiling that truncates mid-sentence when a Cyrillic-
heavy translation turn overruns (Cyrillic costs several tokens per word). 1.5 inherited all of it.

So 1.6 is **1.3's teaching body, verbatim**, with only the turn-taking rewritten. Not 1.5 with
longer chunks — 1.3, plus 1.5's mechanical pacing pair.

### 1.2 What ask (1) actually is, mechanically

A conversational agent has no "keep narrating" mode. After it finishes a turn it is silent until
either the learner speaks or **`turn_timeout`** fires — the platform's *"maximum wait time for the
user's reply before re-engaging the user"*. So "wait a little and move forward" is not a prompt
instruction at all; it is that timer. The prompt only controls **what** it says when the timer
fires, which is why 1.5 states "silence means continue" as prominently as anything else.

1.3 inherits `DEFAULT_TURN_TIMEOUT_SECONDS = 7` (`apps/web/src/agent/prompts/index.ts`). Seven
seconds of dead air reads as *waiting for you*. 1.5 pinned **3**, which is also
`MIN_TURN_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` — the floor `effectiveConfig` enforces, because the mobile held pause
keeps a paused lesson quiet by resetting this same timer every `TUTOR_HEARTBEAT_MS = 1000`
(`packages/shared/src/tutor.ts`).

**Decided: `turnTimeoutSeconds: 3`** — the floor, and the value words-1.5 already runs at. Four was
proposed for the extra second of margin; three was chosen because on a version whose turns are
*long* (see §1.4) the gaps are rarer, so each one has to read unambiguously as a breath rather than
a wait. Sitting exactly on `MIN_TURN_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` means a held pause survives losing one
heartbeat ping (2 s < 3 s) but not two — and two consecutive losses on a live data channel mean the
line is in trouble, which the drop path owns, not a faster ping. Anything lower requires lowering
`TUTOR_HEARTBEAT_MS` first and redoing that arithmetic, not editing the floor.

**`turnEagerness: "patient"` comes with it, not optionally.** A short timeout without it is a tutor
that talks over a learner who paused mid-sentence hunting for an English word — which, for someone
composing aloud, is constant. The two knobs are one decision (types.ts already says so).

### 1.3 What ask (2) removes from 1.3's text

Every place 1.3 hands the floor to the learner:

| Where in 1.3 | What it says | 1.6 |
|---|---|---|
| SOUND thread | "Invite the learner to say it back when it's worth it." | say it once at speed, once slowly, move on — no invitation |
| Session flow | "Teach one item at a time, then check in ('want to go deeper…?') before moving on." | announce the next item and continue; never ask permission |
| Interruptions | "briefly offer to continue where you left off ('shall I finish this one, or move on?')" | answer, then return to teaching with two words of re-orientation |
| USAGE | (no explicit ask, but "usage traps" invites quizzing) | unchanged; already narration |
| Recall recap | "**ask** the learner to bring back two or three of the items from memory" | narrate the recap: name it, leave a beat, answer your own cue |

Kept verbatim, because ask (2) is about *the learner producing speech*, not about the tutor's own
mouth: **"When a word is easy to mishear or spell, spell it out letter by letter"** stays. The
tutor spelling `S-E-I-Z-E` aloud is input, not a spelling test.

One question survives, and it must: *"If the interruption is unclear or you didn't catch it, ask
them to repeat."* That is the one thing the tutor cannot continue without. 1.5 already carves this
exception explicitly and 1.6 should copy the wording.

### 1.4 The trade-off to accept out loud

1.3 has **no `maxTokens`** and no turn budget, so one turn is a 200–300-word weave — 80–120 seconds
of speech. That was 1.4's whole complaint, and it is not only a teaching complaint:

> Pause can never be finer-grained than one turn: the ElevenLabs client→server protocol has no
> abort, so a Pause pressed mid-monologue leaves the rest of it playing to a silenced speaker.

Choosing 1.3 as the base **re-accepts that cost**. The mitigations already shipped and they are not
nothing: `holdSession` sends `PAUSE_STOP_MESSAGE` when `isSpeaking` (a real barge-in that ends the
turn server-side), `setAgentAudioVolume(…, 0)` silences the speaker locally and instantly, and
`releaseSession` asks for the *tail* of the aborted thought rather than the whole item
(`ABORTED_RESUME_MESSAGE`). So a pause mid-monologue is silent immediately and costs one bounded
restatement on resume.

**Recommendation: leave `maxTokens` unset on 1.6**, matching 1.3. Adding 1.4's 220-token ceiling
without 1.4's four-sentence prompt budget would just truncate 1.3's weave mid-word and hand TTS a
fragment — a limiter with no prompt behind it is strictly worse than none. If long turns turn out to
be the problem after all, the next version adds a *stated* budget and a backstop together, the way
1.4 did.

### 1.5 Registry mechanics

- New module `apps/web/src/agent/prompts/words-1.6.ts`, imported into `PROMPT_VERSIONS` **last** —
  the array is ordered oldest → newest and *the last entry is the UI default* (`index.ts`). So
  adding it makes 1.6 the default for new sessions, and 1.4/1.5 stay selectable in the picker.
- `ttsModelId: "eleven_v3_conversational"` and `additionalLanguages: ["ru"]` carry over — Russian in
  the translation moment needs a Russian-capable TTS model.
- Then `pnpm sync:agents` and **commit `apps/web/src/agent/agents.lock.json`** — clients read the
  agent id from that committed file via `lib/agent-registry.ts`, so an un-synced version is a
  version nobody can start.
- `effectiveConfig` will assert `turnTimeoutSeconds >= 3` at sync time. 4 passes.

Nothing about 1.4 or 1.5 needs deleting. They are pinned agents that behave as they were pinned;
retiring one is a separate decision and costs an agent id.

## §2 — The End session button

### 2.1 What the button actually runs

`apps/mobile/src/app/lessons/[id]/index.tsx:1185`

```tsx
<Button label="End session" onPress={() => void endWithPersist()} />
```

```ts
const endWithPersist = useCallback(async () => {
  if (statusRef.current !== "connected") return;
  await persistSession();     // ← network
  endSession();               // ← the hangup
}, [persistSession, endSession]);
```

And `persistSession` is:

```ts
await apiFetch(API_V2_ROUTES.lessonSession, accessToken, { method: "POST", body: … });
await clearJournal(lessonId);
void load();
```

### 2.2 The cause: the hangup is behind an untimed network call

`apiFetch` (`apps/mobile/src/api.ts`) does two awaits before anything else can happen:

```ts
const token = await getToken();                 // Auth0 getCredentials() — may hit the network
const res = await fetch(`${env.apiBaseUrl}${path}`, …);   // no AbortSignal, no timeout
```

`getToken` is `getCredentials()` from `react-native-auth0`, and its docblock in `api.ts` is explicit
that it is called per request precisely *because* it is the call that silently renews an expired
token — i.e. it is a network round trip whenever the access token has aged out, which mid-lesson it
routinely has. Neither that call nor the `fetch` carries a timeout, and React Native's `fetch` has
no default one.

So on a weak or captive network — exactly the conditions a 20-minute voice lesson runs into — the
learner presses **End session** and:

- `endSession()` is never reached, for as long as the request hangs;
- the WebRTC session stays up, the tutor keeps teaching, the meter keeps running;
- the button re-renders identically (no `disabled`, no busy label), so there is no feedback that
  anything was pressed;
- pressing again re-enters the same `await` — and worse, `persistSession` has already set
  `savedForRef.current = conversationId`, so the second call returns *immediately at the guard* and
  the second press ends the session while the first press looks like it did nothing.

That last detail is why the symptom is "does not work **for some reason**" rather than "is always
broken": at the start of a lesson `persistSession` short-circuits on `linesRef.current.length === 0`
and End is instant; once there is a transcript, End is only as fast as the network.

### 2.3 The persist-first ordering is stale reasoning

`endWithPersist`'s docblock justifies the ordering as:

> **End, the way the lock screen must do it: persist, THEN hang up.** … a card tap can arrive with
> the app in the background and nothing on screen …

**There is no lock-screen End any more.** It was removed on 2026-08-18: `ResolvedIntents`
(`lib/lesson-activity-state.ts`) carries only `togglePause` and `toggleMute`, and `FooterRow`
(`targets/controls/LessonActivityView.swift`) renders a `Link`, never an End button. The only caller
of `endWithPersist` today is the on-screen button — pressed with the app in the foreground, on a
mounted screen, by a learner who can see what they are ending.

Persisting is not lost by hanging up first, because it is wired **three** ways already:

1. `onDisconnect` calls `void persistSession()` on every disconnect, including `reason: "user"`;
2. the on-disk journal is written on every `onMessage`, so a save that fails survives a force-quit;
3. the ElevenLabs post-call webhook writes the same `conversation_id` row server-side.

### 2.4 The fix

```ts
/**
 * End, from the screen. The hangup is FIRST and is not behind a network call: `apiFetch` has no
 * timeout, and a pressed End that waits on a token refresh is a lesson that keeps talking and
 * keeps billing with nothing on screen explaining why. `onDisconnect` reaches `persistSession`
 * anyway, and the journal is on disk; this second call is the belt to that pair of braces and the
 * per-conversation guard makes it a no-op when it loses the race.
 */
const endWithPersist = useCallback(() => {
  endSession();
  void persistSession();
}, [persistSession, endSession]);
```

Three parts, and each is separately worth having:

- **Drop the `statusRef.current !== "connected"` early return.** It silently swallows a press in
  every state that is not exactly `"connected"` — including `"disconnecting"`, which the screen's
  `onStatusChange` records but `ConversationStatusProvider` deliberately filters out, so the button
  can be visible (`connected === true`) while the ref says otherwise. `BaseConversation.endSession`
  already no-ops safely when its own status is neither connected nor connecting, so the guard buys
  nothing and can only lose presses.
- **End first.** Above.
- **Acknowledge the press.** Even at its new speed the button should switch to a disabled
  `Ending…` for the tick before `status` flips, so a press is never invisible. This is the part that
  would have made the original bug reportable as "End hangs" rather than "End does nothing".

While in there: `apiFetch` should grow an `AbortSignal.timeout(…)` on the `fetch` and a bounded race
around `getToken()`. That is a separate, broader change — every screen shares it — and it is the
right general fix, but it is **not** the fix for this button. A button whose correctness depends on
a network timeout being short enough is still a button behind the network.

### 2.5 Confirming it on device before shipping

The cause above is read off the code and is sufficient on its own, but it costs one line to prove:
log around the two awaits in `endWithPersist` and press End on airplane mode mid-lesson. If the
first log lands and the second does not, it is §2.2. If neither lands, the press is not reaching
the handler and the investigation moves to `Button`. Worth doing, because "does not work for some
reason" is a report, not a diagnosis, and the fix should not depend on my having guessed right.

## §3 — The data gap behind (4), (6-adjacent) and (7)

### 3.1 What the client has today

`GET /api/v2/lessons/:id/items` returns `LessonItem[]`:

```ts
export interface LessonItem {
  id: string;          // the lesson_items row id — what removeItem needs
  text: string;        // embedded from words(text)
  position: number;
  created_at: string;
  removed_at: string | null;
}
```

No word id, no translation. And `LessonDetail` — the other payload the screen holds — carries
`itemsDetailed: TutorItem[]`, which is `{ text, details }`: it *has* the translations but has no ids
either, by design (`api.ts` says so explicitly: neither `items` nor `itemsDetailed` "can address a
row").

So today the lesson screen cannot link a word to `/lesson-items/:id` (that route's `:id` is the
**`words` row id**, per `ItemRow.id`), and the lock-screen card cannot show a translation.

### 3.2 The change

`lesson_items.word_id` exists and is `not null` since migration `0007_words_m2m.sql`, and
`listLessonItemHistory` already embeds `words(text)`. So:

```ts
// apps/web/src/lib/lessons.ts — listLessonItemHistory
.select("id, position, created_at, removed_at, word_id, words(text, details)")
```

and map `details?.translations_ru?.[0] ?? null`.

```ts
// packages/shared/src/lesson-types.ts
export interface LessonItem {
  id: string;
  /** The `words` row id — what `/lesson-items/:id` addresses. Null on an optimistic row: word
   *  identity needs Postgres (`resolve_words`), so the client cannot know it before the re-read. */
  wordId: string | null;
  text: string;
  /** First entry of `words.details.translations_ru`, or null when the word is not enriched yet. */
  translationRu: string | null;
  position: number;
  created_at: string;
  removed_at: string | null;
}
```

**Both new fields are nullable, and that is not defensive slop.** The optimistic row built by
`addItems` mints only the `lesson_items` id (`buildAddItemsOp` + `newId`); the word id comes from
the `resolve_words` RPC server-side, and the translation from the enrichment job that runs *after*
the write. Nullable is the honest shape, and it is the shape the UI has to render anyway: a
just-added word is plain text with no translation until `load()` returns, which is one round trip
later.

**The first two, comma-joined — not one, and not the array.** `translations_ru` is documented as
"best/most-common first", and one Russian word routinely covers only one shade of an English one:
`ephemeral` is мимолётный *or* недолговечный depending on what is being described, and showing only
the first quietly asserts a precision the data does not have. Two is where that stops being a
problem and before the card's single line starts truncating. Capping in the mapper rather than
sending the array keeps one rule on the server instead of a slice per client; the full list — with
forms, examples and their Russian — is already the word detail page's job, and that page is now one
tap away, which is exactly ask (7).

`isLessonItemsResponse` narrows on `Array.isArray(body.items)` only, so no narrowing change is
needed and an older client reading a newer server keeps working. `apps/web/src/app/lessons/[id]/page.tsx`
consumes `LessonItem[]` for its change log and touches neither new field.

## §4 — The lock-screen card (asks 4, 5, 6)

### 4.1 Where the words come from now

```ts
// apps/mobile/src/app/lessons/[id]/index.tsx
const activityWords = useMemo(() => active.map((item) => item.text), [active]);
```
→ `buildActivityState` → `windowWords` (`ACTIVITY_WORD_WINDOW = 6`) → `ActivityState.words: string[]`
→ `LessonActivityAttributes.ContentState.words: [String]` → `WordGrid`, two columns.

### 4.2 Getting translations across the wire

Two shapes are possible and the choice matters more than it looks:

**A. Keep `words: [String]`, pre-join in JS** — `"ephemeral — мимолётный"` — and let Swift add the
`1.` from `ForEach(Array(words.enumerated()))`, which it already does.

**B. Change `ContentState.words` to a `[WordLine]` struct** with `text` and `ru`.

**Decided: A.** `ContentState` is `Codable` and is compiled into *both* targets; changing its shape
changes the decode contract for any activity the system is still holding from a previous build.
`reconcileAtLaunch` ends every surviving activity at launch, so the window is small — but it is not
zero, and the payoff for B is a formatting decision that JS is already the right place for. It also
keeps `sameActivityState`'s cheap `words.every(...)` comparison and every existing `check.ts`
assertion valid unchanged. The one thing A gives up is Swift being able to style the translation
differently (dimmer, smaller) — worth it, and reversible later if the card looks flat.

So:

```ts
/** One line: the word, and its Russian when we have one. The em-dash join lives here rather than
 *  in Swift because the whole point of ACTIVITY_WORD_WINDOW living in JS is that the window rule
 *  has one home; the same argument applies to the line rule. Shared with the lesson panel (§5). */
export function itemLine(item: { text: string; translationRu: string | null }): string {
  return item.translationRu ? `${item.text} — ${item.translationRu}` : item.text;
}
```

`activityWords` becomes `active.map(itemLine)`, and `windowWords` / `overflow` are untouched.

### 4.3 One per line, numbered (ask 4)

`WordGrid` → `WordList`: a `VStack(alignment: .leading, spacing: 3)` over
`Array(words.enumerated())`, each `Text("\(index + 1). \(word)")`, `.lineLimit(1)` with
`.truncationMode(.tail)`. The `focusIndex` bolding survives as-is (still `nil` — nothing computes a
current word yet, §5.2 of the 2026-08-16 doc).

### 4.4 The height budget, which is now the binding constraint

> **Corrected 2026-08-20, after review.** The arithmetic below originally read "≈ 149 pt, it fits"
> and it was wrong twice: it costed a `.subheadline` row at 18 pt when the line height is ~20, and
> it omitted the VStack's own spacing between children entirely. The decision to hold
> `ACTIVITY_WORD_WINDOW` at 6 (§7) was taken against that wrong number. The real figures are below.

The card is laid out against a ~160 pt cap. At default Dynamic Type:

```
  24  vertical padding (12 × 2)
  18  header row (.footnote)
  16  two VStack gaps (header→list, list→footer)
 130  six .subheadline rows (6 × 20 + 5 × 2 internal spacing)
----
 188  — and 206 once the truncation notice is showing
```

Five rows is ~166 / 184; **four is ~144 / 162, and four is the only count this arithmetic
supports.** Asks 4, 5 and 6 are still one change — the ~30 pt the status line gave back and the
~26 pt the footer gave back are real, and without them the list would be worse still — they just do
not add up to six rows.

Two consequences, and they are separable:

1. **The window number is now an open question again**, not a settled one. §7 records the decision
   as taken; it needs re-taking against these numbers.
2. **The truncation notice must not be the thing that gets truncated.** It used to be the last
   element of a VStack that overflows its container, which made it the first thing clipped — so the
   invariant its own comment states ("a cut list that does not say it was cut reads as a wrong
   list") held only while the card happened to fit. It moved above the rows and is worded as a
   count ("First 6 of 10"), which is a header rather than a tail. That fix stands whatever the
   window number turns out to be, and it makes overflowing degrade into a missing *word* — which
   the header has already accounted for — rather than a list silently claiming to be whole.

**Decided: `ACTIVITY_WORD_WINDOW` holds at 6, and gets measured on a device.** It has never been
measured on one — the constant's
own comment says six "stays at six until probe #3 measures the real cap … because guessing upward is
how a card starts clipping its last row" — and this change makes each row taller, so if anything the
measurement is now overdue. `overflow` already renders "+N more", so a reduction to 5 is a one-line
change that stays honest.

### 4.5 Status beside the title (ask 6)

```swift
HStack(alignment: .firstTextBaseline) {
  Text(state.title).font(.footnote.weight(.medium)).lineLimit(1)
  Spacer(minLength: 8)
  Text(statusChip).font(.caption).foregroundStyle(.secondary).lineLimit(1).layoutPriority(1)
}
```

**The current sentences cannot go there.** "⏸ Paused — microphone muted, but the tutor may still be
audible" is 60 characters; beside a lesson title it truncates to "⏸ Paused — microph…". So (6) needs
a second, compact vocabulary:

| phase | compact chip |
|---|---|
| `.live` | `● Listening` |
| `.muted` | `🎤 Muted` |
| `.held`, silenced | `⏸ Paused` |
| `.held`, **not** silenced | `⏸ Paused` **+ keep the long line below** |
| `.over` | `Ended` |

The last row is the part not to lose. The two-variant paused sentence exists as a **disclosure**:
`silenced: false` means the feature-detected reach into LiveKit that silences the tutor has broken
and *the tutor is still audible through a pause*. §7.6 of the 2026-08-16 doc is explicit that on a
locked screen that failure **is** the experience, so the card says so rather than claiming a quiet it
did not deliver. Compressing it to "⏸ Paused" would turn a working disclosure into a lie.

**Proposal:** the chip is always on the title line; when `phase == .held && !silenced` a single extra
caption line appears beneath it ("the tutor may still be audible"). It costs ~13 pt in the one state
that has earned it, and that state is rare by construction — it only happens after an SDK upgrade
breaks the escape hatch.

Also worth pulling out: the compact vocabulary duplicates `nowPlayingSubtitle`'s job in a third
place (Swift card, Swift chip, JS Now Playing). The split is already deliberate — the widget
extension renders with no JS process, so its copy must be in Swift — so this is a note, not a
refactor.

### 4.6 Removing the controls hint (ask 5) — and what it costs

The line being deleted is:

> "Add the Pause and Mute controls to your Lock Screen to use them without unlocking"

Its docblock is unusually candid about why it exists:

> It is here because Apple gives an app no way to install its own control, so the only lever left is
> telling the learner the controls exist and where — and this card is the one surface guaranteed to
> be in front of them mid-lesson. Honest about being a mitigation rather than a fix.

Delete it and the two Controls — the **only** surface that can pause a lesson on a *locked* device,
since Apple deactivates every button in every Live Activity until unlock — become undiscoverable to
anyone who has not already found them. There is no API that reports whether the learner added them,
so nothing can detect the regression either.

The ask is right about the space, and the space is what (4) needs. **Decided: delete it outright**,
with no replacement in the app. One sentence against six translations, and the sentence never was a
fix — its own docblock calls it a mitigation and points at the Now Playing surface (§1.7 of the
2026-08-18 doc) as the real answer, which ships already and needs no setup at all.

What that costs, stated so it is inherited deliberately rather than by accident: a learner who has
not already added the two Controls has no in-product path to discovering them, and since there is no
API that reports whether they were added, nothing can detect that. The lever if it ever matters is
Now Playing, not another line of copy on a card.

## §5 — The lesson page's Words panel (ask 7)

Current row (`index.tsx:1097–1109`): `<Body>{item.text}</Body>` + a text `Button` labelled
`remove`, wired straight to `removeItem` with **no confirmation**.

Target row, one per line, numbered:

```
1. ephemeral — мимолётный                                    ✕
2. break the ice — растопить лёд                             ✕
```

Four pieces:

**The link.** `<Link href={`/lesson-items/${item.wordId}`} variant="plain">{item.text}</Link>` when
`wordId` is non-null; plain `<Body>` when it is null (an optimistic row, for the one round trip
before `load()` returns). `Link`'s `href` is typed by expo-router's `typedRoutes`, so the route is
checked at compile time. `variant="plain"` is the right one — the same choice the lessons list makes
for a title that is a link but reads as content, and six accent-blue words in a row is a panel that
looks like a link farm.

**The number and the translation.** Same rule as the card, and it is the *same function* —
`itemLine` from §4.2 is not lock-screen-specific, it is "how this app writes a word with its
translation", and having the two surfaces drift is exactly the class of bug `packages/shared` exists
to prevent. The number comes from the render index (the row order is `active`, sorted by `position`,
which is also the order handed to the tutor as `{{items_list}}` — so the numbering on screen matches
the numbering the tutor teaches by, which is a small free win).

Style note: the translation should be `Muted`-toned inline rather than a second line. `ItemLine` in
the collection already does exactly this shape (bold word, muted stats underneath); here the
translation is short enough to sit on the same line, and a second line per word doubles a panel that
can hold `MAX_ITEMS` rows.

**The ✕ icon.** `src/ui/icons.tsx` has Star, SortArrow, Sun, Moon, ChevronDown, Check, Refresh and
Trash — **no X**. Add one:

```tsx
/** A cross — remove from a collection, NOT delete. Deliberately not `TrashIcon`: the trash means
 *  the word is gone from the account (`/lesson-items` and the word page use it for exactly that),
 *  and a lesson removal keeps the word, its practice history and its membership in every other
 *  lesson. Two different outcomes must not share a glyph. */
export function CloseIcon(props: IconProps) { … }
```

That distinction is the reason to be careful here: the collection's trash icon calls `deleteWord`,
which the confirm dialog warns "leaves every lesson and loses its practice history and translation".
The lesson panel's ✕ calls `removeItem` → `{ kind: "removeItem" }`, which soft-deletes one
`lesson_items` row and keeps everything else — *and* keeps the row itself, because the row is what
feeds the "Word changes" log below. Same-looking control, opposite blast radius.

Render it with `<Button variant="icon" tone="danger" hitSlop={4}>` and
`accessibilityLabel={`Remove ${item.text} from this lesson`}` — the pattern `ItemLine` already uses,
including the deliberately small `hitSlop` when a destructive control has neighbours.

**The confirmation.** `ConfirmDialog` is controlled and the codebase's stated pattern is *one dialog
for the whole list, driven by which row is pending* — so a single `const [confirmTarget, setConfirmTarget] = useState<LessonItem | null>(null)`,
not one dialog per row. Copy has to match the actual outcome, the way the two existing dialogs
deliberately differ from each other:

> **Remove “ephemeral” from this lesson?**
> It stays in your collection with its practice history, and you can add it back any time.

Reassuring, not warning — because unlike the collection's delete, this one really is that cheap.

One caveat to keep from the existing panel: `Changes apply to your next conversation.` stays. It is
true of removals as much as additions — `items_list` is baked into `dynamicVariables` at connect —
and it is the sentence that stops a removal mid-lesson reading as broken.

## §8 — What shipped

Everything above, on `master`, 2026-08-20.

**words-1.6** — `apps/web/src/agent/prompts/words-1.6.ts`, appended last to `PROMPT_VERSIONS` so it
is the UI default. `pnpm sync:agents` created exactly one agent
(`agent_3901m0g9a9bzfjd9sejmt993vcng`) and reported `no-op` for 1.0–1.5, so no previously pinned
agent was re-PATCHed. `agents.lock.json` is committed.

**End** — `endWithPersist` no longer awaits anything: `endSession()` first, `void persistSession()`
after, no `statusRef` guard, and an `Ending…` disabled state so a press is never invisible. The flag
is cleared in `start()` rather than in a `status` effect — the button only renders while
`connected`, so there is nothing to clear on the way down and a setState in an effect body would be
paying for a state nobody can see.

**The card** — `WordGrid` → `WordList` (one numbered line per word, index from the render position);
title and a compact `statusChip` share one `HStack` with the chip at higher `layoutPriority`; the
`silenced == false` disclosure survives as its own caption line, which is the one state where prose
is load-bearing; `FooterRow` renders nothing during a live session. The Dynamic Island's expanded
region takes four rows instead of six and folds the difference into `overflow`.

**The data** — `LessonItem` gained `wordId` and `translationRu`, both nullable, plus
`ITEM_TRANSLATION_LIMIT` and `itemLine`. `listLessonItemHistory` selects `word_id, words(text,
details)` and joins the first two translations server-side.

**The panel** — a `WordRow` component: number, spelling as a `plain` link to `/lesson-items/:wordId`
(plain text while `wordId` is null, i.e. for the one round trip after an optimistic add), the Russian
in muted type, and a `CloseIcon` that opens one shared `ConfirmDialog`. `CloseIcon` is new and is
deliberately not `TrashIcon` — the bin deletes a word from the account, this cross removes one
`lesson_items` row.
